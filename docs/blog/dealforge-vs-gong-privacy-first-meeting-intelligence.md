# DealForge vs Gong: Why Privacy-First Meeting Intelligence Matters

Gong built the category. Its recording, transcription, and analytics platform is the default answer to "how do we get intelligence out of our sales calls?" — and for good reason. It's polished, it's integrated, and it's become a verb.

But there's a structural difference between Gong and a new generation of privacy-first tools like DealForge, and it's not a feature comparison. It's an architecture comparison: **Gong stores every word of your calls on its servers. DealForge keeps them in your browser.**

Here's an honest comparison — where Gong wins, where the local-first approach wins, and why the privacy question is the one that should decide your stack.

## What Gong does well

Gong is excellent at what it does. Full credit where it's due:

- **Team-scale analytics.** Aggregate call intelligence across an entire revenue org — talk tracks, competitor mentions, win/loss patterns — is genuinely powerful.
- **Deep integrations.** Salesforce, Slack, and the rest of the enterprise stack, wired by a team that's been doing it for years.
- **Mature process.** Tried-and-true onboarding, training, and rollout playbooks for sales leadership.

If you're a large enterprise team and you've already decided your call data lives in a vendor's cloud, Gong is a defensible choice. The question is whether that decision was ever on the table.

## Where the architecture differs

### Gong: cloud-native, data custodianship included

Gong's value proposition depends on centralizing your data — every call, every rep, every word — into their platform to mine it for patterns. The consequences:

- Your pricing, your prospect list, and your internal deal commentary live on their servers, subject to their security posture and their terms of service.
- Your conversations become part of their analytics corpus.
- "Deleting your data" is a legal process defined by their policy, not a technical instant action.
- Every new AI capability they ship is, implicitly, another way your data is processed on their infrastructure.

### DealForge: local-first, BYOK

DealForge delivers the same core meeting workflow — transcription, AI summaries, action items, lead scoring, pipeline tracking, and automated email follow-up — with an inverted architecture:

- Transcripts and analyses are stored in your browser's local database. There is no DealForge server holding your meeting content.
- AI analysis runs through **your own API keys** (OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini). You choose the provider, you hold the key, you see the prompts.
- "Delete all data" deletes all data — instantly, locally, unconditionally.
- Your meeting intelligence works offline.

## The question Gong can't answer

Gong's entire business is built on aggregating meeting data. When you ask Gong "will you guarantee, technically and contractually, that none of our calls ever touch your infrastructure or improve your models?" — the answer can't be what a local-first tool can say: **"the data never reaches us at all."**

That's not a feature gap. It's a structural moat in reverse. Your meeting data is your competitive advantage — your sales motion, your pricing strategy, your honest assessments. A tool that stores it on its own servers becomes a counterparty to that information. A local-first tool is just a tool.

## Honest tradeoffs of local-first

To be fair: local-first has real tradeoffs at team scale. Team-wide analytics across 50 reps requires shared data — which is why DealForge's enterprise tier is explicitly on the roadmap rather than claimed today. If your #1 requirement is org-wide aggregated call analytics, Gong (or a local-first tool after enterprise ships) is the right fit. If your #1 requirement is that your calls stay yours — and for an increasing number of sales leaders, it is — the architecture decides the deal.

## The bottom line

Gong wins on team analytics maturity. DealForge wins on the question that matters increasingly in 2026: who controls your data. For individual reps, small teams, and privacy-conscious sales organizations, local-first meeting intelligence delivers the same day-to-day value — transcription, summaries, scoring, follow-up — without making your calls a vendor's inventory.
