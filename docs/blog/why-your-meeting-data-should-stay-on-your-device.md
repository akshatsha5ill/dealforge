# Why Your Meeting Data Should Stay on Your Device

Your sales calls contain your pricing strategy, your customer list, your honest internal assessment of deals, and your proprietary sales motion. That's exactly the kind of data you'd protect with a locked filing cabinet — yet most sales teams feed every word of it to a third party's servers every single day.

Meeting intelligence is one of the most valuable tools a sales team can adopt. It's also one of the most invasive. Here's why the "where does my data live" question isn't paranoia — it's due diligence — and why local-first architecture is the right answer.

## The default: your transcripts on someone else's servers

Almost every major meeting intelligence platform — Gong, Chorus, Otter.ai, Fireflies — works the same way. Your meeting recording and transcript are uploaded to their cloud, processed by their AI, and stored in their database. That means:

- **Your data is part of their attack surface.** A breach at the vendor is a breach of your pricing page, your deal sizes, your prospect list.
- **Your data trains their models.** Depending on the terms of service, your conversations may improve their product for your competitors.
- **Your data outlives your relationship.** Cancel your subscription and ask for full deletion — in many cases, what "deletion" means is defined by their lawyers, not your security team.
- **Your data is subject to their jurisdiction.** If the vendor operates under foreign data laws, your sales conversations are subject to them too.

None of this is a conspiracy theory. It's the plain mechanics of cloud SaaS. The vendor stores your data because their business model is built on it.

## The alternative: local-first meeting intelligence

A local-first tool flips the architecture. The meeting transcript never leaves your browser. The AI analysis runs against your own API keys (a model called BYOK — Bring Your Own Key). Summaries, action items, lead scores, and email drafts are computed on your machine and stored in your browser's local database.

What this means in practice:

- **No meeting data on vendor servers.** Ever. There's nothing to breach, nothing to subpoena, nothing to train on.
- **Your API keys stay yours.** You bring your own OpenAI, Anthropic, or Gemini key. The vendor isn't brokering your AI usage or logging your prompts.
- **Data deletion is real deletion.** Clear your browser data and everything is gone — immediately, unconditionally.
- **Offline by default.** Your meeting history works even when you're on a plane or in a prospect's dead-zone conference room.

## But local-first is a feature, not a caveat

It's easy to hear "your data stays in your browser" as "it's a less capable tool." It's the opposite. Modern browsers can run real AI workflows locally — the bottleneck isn't the device, it's the software design. A local-first meeting intelligence tool does everything a cloud tool does — transcription, summaries, action items, lead scoring, email drafting — without the data custodianship question.

There's one honest tradeoff: if you use AI analysis, your chosen AI provider (OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google) still sees the transcript you send them. BYOK makes that relationship explicit and controlled by you — you choose the provider, you hold the key, you can stop anytime. That's a very different position from "the platform vendor decides who sees your calls."

## What to ask before you buy

- **Where is my transcript stored?** If the answer is "our secure cloud," ask for their breach history, data residency, and deletion policy.
- **Do you train on my data?** If the answer isn't a plain "no, ever," treat it as yes.
- **Can I export or delete everything on demand?** Local-first tools can answer yes without legal review.
- **Who processes my AI analysis?** Bring-your-own-key means you control the entire AI supply chain.

## The competitive angle

There's a reason Gong and Chorus compete on features rather than data handling: privacy-first architecture is expensive for them to match. Their moat is your data. Your advantage is refusing to hand it over.
